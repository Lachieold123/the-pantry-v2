## 1. Thai Jungle Curry with Pork and Green Beans

*A hot, brothy red curry with no coconut milk, full of beans, eggplant, lime leaves and Thai basil.*

thai · dinner · easy · prep 15 min, cook 20 min · serves 4 · diets: no-gluten, no-dairy

Per serve (estimate): 260 kcal, protein 30 g, carbs 12 g, fat 9 g, fibre 4 g, sodium 1450 mg

**Ingredients**

- 1 tbsp neutral oil
- 3 tbsp red curry paste
- 20g galangal, thinly sliced
- 500g pork fillet, thinly sliced
- 750ml chicken stock
- 2 Asian eggplants, cut into 3cm chunks
- 6 makrut lime leaves, torn
- 200g green beans, cut into 4cm lengths
- 2 tbsp fish sauce
- 2 tsp grated palm sugar
- 1 handful Thai basil leaves
- Steamed jasmine rice, to serve

**Method**

1. Heat the oil in a wok or large saucepan over medium heat. Add the curry paste and galangal and stir for 2 minutes, until the paste darkens a shade and smells fragrant.
2. Add the pork and stir-fry for 2 minutes, until every slice is coated in paste and no longer looks raw on the outside.
3. Pour in the stock, add the eggplant and lime leaves and bring to a simmer. Cook for 8 minutes, until the eggplant is just tender.
4. Add the beans and simmer for 4 minutes more, until they are bright green and tender-crisp and the pork is cooked through with no pink in the middle.
5. Season with the fish sauce and palm sugar, then taste: it should be hot, salty and herby rather than sweet. Stir in the basil until it just wilts and serve with jasmine rice.

**Notes**

- Food safety: cook the pork until it reaches 70°C in the centre. Thin slices get there in a few minutes of simmering.
- Jungle curry has no coconut milk, so it is thinner and hotter than a red curry. Use 2 tbsp paste for a milder pot.
- Most red curry paste contains shrimp paste. Check the label if you're cooking for someone who avoids shellfish.

## 2. Vegan Lemon and Olive Oil Loaf Cake

*A tender, lemony loaf made with olive oil and soy milk, finished with a sharp lemon glaze.*

modern-australian · snack · easy · prep 15 min, cook 50 min · serves 10 · diets: vegetarian, vegan, no-dairy

Per serve (estimate): 340 kcal, protein 4 g, carbs 54 g, fat 12 g, fibre 1 g, sodium 220 mg

**Ingredients**

*Cake*

- 200g caster sugar
- Zest of 2 lemons, finely grated
- 300g plain flour
- 2 tsp baking powder
- 1/2 tsp bicarbonate of soda
- 1/4 tsp salt
- 250ml soy milk
- 125ml mild olive oil
- 60ml lemon juice
- 1 tbsp apple cider vinegar

*Glaze*

- 120g icing sugar
- 1 1/2 tbsp lemon juice

**Method**

1. Heat the oven to 180°C (160°C fan). Grease a 22 x 11cm loaf tin and line the base and long sides with baking paper.
2. Rub the lemon zest into the caster sugar with your fingertips in a large bowl until it smells strongly of lemon. Whisk in the flour, baking powder, bicarbonate of soda and salt.
3. In a jug, whisk the soy milk, olive oil, lemon juice and vinegar. The milk will thicken and curdle a little, which is what you want.
4. Pour the wet mix into the dry and fold with a spatula until no dry flour shows. Stop there: a few lumps are fine, and overmixing makes a tough loaf.
5. Scrape into the tin and bake for 45 to 50 minutes, until risen and golden and a skewer pushed into the middle comes out clean. Cool in the tin for 10 minutes, then lift onto a rack and cool completely.
6. Stir the icing sugar with the lemon juice until smooth and thick enough to coat a spoon. Spoon over the cold loaf and leave it to set for 15 minutes before slicing.

**Notes**

- Soy milk works best because its protein thickens with the vinegar. Other unsweetened plant milks work but give a slightly flatter loaf.
- Keeps in an airtight tin for 3 days. It is moist enough not to need butter.

## 3. Rigatoni with Cavolo Nero, White Beans and Chilli Crumbs

*A 30-minute vegan pasta: garlicky greens and creamy beans, with crisp chilli breadcrumbs on top.*

italian · lunch, dinner · easy · prep 10 min, cook 20 min · serves 4 · diets: vegetarian, vegan, no-dairy

Per serve (estimate): 645 kcal, protein 20 g, carbs 91 g, fat 20 g, fibre 9 g, sodium 800 mg

**Ingredients**

- 400g rigatoni
- 80ml extra virgin olive oil
- 60g fresh breadcrumbs
- 1/2 tsp chilli flakes
- 4 garlic cloves, thinly sliced
- 1 bunch cavolo nero (about 250g), stems removed, leaves shredded
- 400g tin cannellini beans, drained and rinsed
- Zest of 1 lemon
- 2 tbsp lemon juice
- 1 tsp salt
- 1/2 tsp freshly ground black pepper

**Method**

1. Bring a large pot of water to the boil and salt it well. Cook the rigatoni for 2 minutes less than the packet says, until it is still firm in the centre.
2. While the pasta cooks, warm 2 tbsp of the oil in a large frying pan over medium heat. Fry the breadcrumbs with half the chilli flakes and a pinch of the salt for 4 minutes, stirring, until golden and crisp. Tip into a bowl and wipe out the pan.
3. Add the rest of the oil to the pan with the garlic and the remaining chilli. Cook gently for 2 minutes, until the garlic is pale gold and fragrant; don't let it brown.
4. Add the cavolo nero and beans with a ladle of pasta water. Cover and cook for 4 minutes, until the leaves have wilted and turned dark and silky.
5. Scoop out a mugful of pasta water, then drain the rigatoni and add it to the pan with the lemon zest, lemon juice, pepper and the rest of the salt. Toss over the heat for 2 minutes, adding splashes of pasta water, until the sauce turns glossy and clings to the pasta.
6. Taste for salt and lemon, then serve straight away with the chilli crumbs scattered over.

**Notes**

- Kale or silverbeet work in place of cavolo nero; add silverbeet stems with the garlic.
- Keep the crumbs separate until serving or they go soft.

## 4. Red-Braised Beef with Daikon

*Beef chuck slow-braised in soy, Shaoxing wine, star anise and rock sugar until it falls apart.*

chinese · dinner · medium · prep 20 min, cook 150 min · serves 6 · diets: no-dairy

Per serve (estimate): 490 kcal, protein 39 g, carbs 10 g, fat 32 g, fibre 2 g, sodium 850 mg

**Ingredients**

- 1.2kg beef chuck, cut into 4cm pieces
- 2 tbsp neutral oil
- 6 spring onions, cut into 5cm lengths
- 40g ginger, sliced
- 6 garlic cloves, smashed
- 3 star anise
- 1 cinnamon stick
- 80ml Shaoxing wine
- 60ml light soy sauce
- 2 tbsp dark soy sauce
- 30g rock sugar (or 2 tbsp brown sugar)
- 1 litre water
- 600g daikon, peeled and cut into 3cm chunks
- Steamed rice, to serve

**Method**

1. Put the beef in a large heavy pot, cover with cold water and bring to the boil. Boil for 3 minutes, until grey scum rises, then drain and rinse the beef and the pot. This keeps the sauce clear.
2. Heat the oil in the clean pot over medium-high heat. Fry the spring onion, ginger and garlic for 1 minute until fragrant, then add the beef and stir for 3 minutes, until browned in places.
3. Add the star anise and cinnamon, pour in the Shaoxing wine and let it bubble for 30 seconds. Stir in both soy sauces, the rock sugar and the water, which should just cover the beef.
4. Bring to the boil, then cover, turn the heat to low and simmer gently for 1 hour 45 minutes, stirring now and then, until the beef is nearly tender.
5. Add the daikon, pushing it under the liquid, and simmer uncovered for 40 minutes, until the beef is fork-tender and the daikon is translucent and soft.
6. Lift out the spices. If the sauce is thin, boil it hard for 5 to 10 minutes until it is glossy and coats a spoon. Serve with rice.

**Notes**

- Food safety: braised beef passes a safe 70°C long before it turns tender, so the cue to watch for is a fork sliding in easily. Refrigerate leftovers within 2 hours and reheat until steaming hot all the way through (75°C).
- It tastes even better the next day and keeps in the fridge for 3 days.
- For a gluten-free version, use tamari for both soy sauces and dry sherry for the Shaoxing wine, and check the labels.

## 5. Besan Chilla with Spinach and Tomato

*Savoury chickpea flour pancakes flecked with spinach, tomato, chilli and coriander. Ready in 30 minutes.*

indian · breakfast, lunch · easy · prep 15 min, cook 20 min · serves 4 · diets: vegetarian, vegan, no-gluten, no-dairy

Per serve (estimate): 280 kcal, protein 12 g, carbs 31 g, fat 10 g, fibre 6 g, sodium 460 mg

**Ingredients**

- 200g chickpea flour
- 1/2 tsp ground turmeric
- 1 tsp cumin seeds
- 1/2 tsp ajwain (carom seeds), optional
- 3/4 tsp salt
- 300ml water
- 60g baby spinach, finely chopped
- 1 tomato, finely diced
- 1/2 small red onion, finely diced
- 1 green chilli, finely chopped
- 2 tbsp chopped coriander
- 1 tsp grated ginger
- 2 tbsp neutral oil
- Lemon wedges, to serve

**Method**

1. Whisk the chickpea flour, turmeric, cumin seeds, ajwain and salt in a bowl. Slowly whisk in the water until you have a smooth batter about as thick as pouring cream. Leave it to stand for 10 minutes so the flour hydrates.
2. Stir the spinach, tomato, onion, chilli, coriander and ginger into the batter. If it has thickened, loosen it with a splash of water.
3. Heat a non-stick frying pan over medium heat and brush it with a little oil. Pour in a ladle of batter and quickly spread it with the back of the ladle into a 15cm round.
4. Cook for 2 to 3 minutes, until the top looks dry and the underside is golden with crisp edges. Drizzle a few drops of oil around the edge, flip and cook for 2 minutes more, until both sides are golden and the middle is cooked through.
5. Keep each chilla warm in a low oven while you cook the rest, brushing the pan with oil each time. Serve hot with lemon wedges.

**Notes**

- Chickpea flour has no gluten but is sometimes milled alongside wheat. Buy one labelled gluten-free if you're cooking for someone with coeliac disease.
- Many versions add asafoetida (hing). Most hing powder is cut with wheat flour, so it is left out here.
